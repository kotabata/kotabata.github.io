## About Me

I am a PhD student at the University of Tokyo, working on the development of new STEM imaging techniques. My research interests currently encompass the development and application of atomic-scale thermal vibration imaging by STEM, as well as the integration of computational science.

## Research Focus

I focus on advancing scanning transmission electron microscopy (STEM) techniques to visualize atomic structures and local chemistry. Recently, understanding thermal transport in nanoscale materials such as thermoelectric materials and electronic devices has become crucial. In real materials, phonons are scattered by crystal defect structures such as atomic vacancies, substitutional atoms, and grain boundaries. Therefore, it is necessary to directly measure atomic vibrations in local regions and elucidate the correlation between local atomic structures and thermal transport. However, a method for quantitatively observing thermal vibrations in such local regions has not yet been established. My research aims to develop a method to directly observe atomic vibrations in local regions by combining STEM with segmented and pixelated detectors. My goal is to understand atomic vibrations and their impact on material properties to develop new materials with tailored functionalities.

## Current Projects

- Detailed analysis of electron scattering distributions associated with atomic vibrations using fast segmented and pixelated detectors
- Development of a differentiable STEM image simulation package for parameter optimization and estimation of atomic vibration parameters
- Development of a live application for 4D STEM
